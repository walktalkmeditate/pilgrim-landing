# Spec: /moonpath — Moon + Tide Almanac Entry

**Status:** draft v4 — addresses doc-review rounds 1+2+3, decisions D9-D18 locked, AC renumbered to 22 entries, blue-moon year bug fixed
**Date:** 2026-05-14
**Lineage:** Entry #3 of the contemplative-instrument almanac. Sibling shape to [/sunpath](../../sunpath/) (entry #1, sun) and [/daylight](../../daylight/) (entry #2, walk-time vs daylight). /moonpath is the moon-and-tide counterpart.

**Universal scope:** unlike /daylight (which bakes a fixed list of pilgrimage routes), /moonpath is **coord-driven** — it accepts any lat/lon on Earth and renders. Coastal-port tide section is the only stage-aware element, and it gracefully hides when the user's coord is inland.

## Problem

**Problem:** Sunpath answers "where is the sun, where will it be, how does its arc differ at this latitude over this many years?" There's no equivalent for the moon. Walkers who want a quiet view of tonight's moon — its position, phase, whether there'll be enough moonlight to start at 4am — have to bounce between Stellarium, timeanddate.com, and tide charts. Coastal walkers (Camino Norte, Kumano Iseji, Mont Saint-Michel, Sydney foreshores) ALSO want to know when the tide's in.

**Who feels it:** Pre-dawn walkers, photographers, coastal hikers, kayakers, anyone who plans by moonlight or tide. The "is the moon out tonight?" question is universal, not pilgrim-route-specific — same posture as sunpath, which serves astronomers + travellers + curious humans.

**When:** Night-before planning, or mid-walk re-evaluation. Same use shape as daylight v1.

**Today's workaround:** Stellarium + timeanddate.com + a NOAA tide chart. Three tabs.

**Cost of doing nothing:** The almanac stays a two-entry publication. The lunar math already shipped into `js/sunpath-math.js` during daylight v2 (slice 1) sits half-used — moonrise/moonset/phase has no dedicated viewer. Without a third entry the "almanac as quiet companion publication" thesis (per `almanac_aesthetic.md`) doesn't accumulate.

## In scope (stretched v1 — 8 features)

> *Baseline scope was 5 features (moon-in-sky, phase clock, earthshine, moonlight lux, tide curve). Stretched v1 adds standstill time-machine, apparent-size dial, and ICS export — 8 total. Any feature pushed below this line in future rounds counts as cut from scope, not de-stretched.*


A new page `/moonpath/` mirroring `/sunpath/` in structure and palette. Universal: not pilgrimage-route-bound. Accepts coord + date (URL params for share), defaults to user's geolocation on first load only if they tap a "use my location" button (per the existing D3 explicit-geolocation discipline established in /daylight).

1. **Moon-in-sky widget** — for the user's coord + the current UTC instant: moon altitude (degrees above/below horizon), azimuth (degrees east of north), rendered as a slow arc traced through tonight's hours. Single SVG dome diagram, similar in posture to /sunpath's sun-altitude renderings.

2. **Phase clock ring** — 29.5-day synodic month rendered as a circle. Today's lit fraction as a moving wedge. Phase name (waxing crescent, first quarter, etc.). Cormorant Garamond italic label — the typeface is already loaded site-wide via the shared `<head>` Google Fonts call inherited from /sunpath, so this requires no new font host (preserves AC #17).

3. **Earthshine annotation** — when the moon's **illuminated-disk fraction** `k` (computed per D13 as `(1 − cos(2π · phase)) / 2`, where `phase` is the synodic phase 0..1 from `moonPhaseAtUTC`) is between **0.03 and 0.15** (thin crescent territory), surface a one-line annotation: *"Earthshine visible — the old moon in the new moon's arms."* Quiet, italic, Cormorant. No emoji, no infographic.

4. **Apparent size dial** — tonight's lunar distance vs the long-term mean (perigee 356,500 km / apogee 406,700 km / mean 384,400 km). Render today's apparent angular diameter as a tiny dial relative to mean. **Math:** angular diameter scales as 1/distance, so the percentage offset is `(meanDistance / todayDistance − 1) × 100` (per D16). Annotation text rendered to **one decimal place**: *"Tonight: 4.3% larger than average"* or *"2.1% smaller than average"*. **The word "supermoon" must not appear** anywhere in copy or code (verified by grep in AC #6).

5. **Moonlight lux calculator** — tonight's illuminance at the user's coord, computed from lunar phase + altitude + atmospheric extinction. Render as a quiet brightness ring (a single arc whose width/opacity scales with lux). **Bracket table** for annotation copy (all values approximate; below-bracket values pick the closest bracket and use its prose):

   | Lux range (half-open intervals) | Prose annotation |
   |---|---|
   | `[0.2, ∞)` | *"~X lux — enough to walk a known path."* |
   | `[0.05, 0.2)` | *"~X lux — usable light along an open trail."* |
   | `[0.005, 0.05)` | *"~X lux — barely usable; carry a headlamp."* |
   | `[0, 0.005)` | *"~X lux — effectively dark; headlamp required."* |

   Boundaries are half-open (left-inclusive, right-exclusive) so each lux value falls in exactly one bracket — deterministic for AC #7. **Moon-below-horizon case:** when `moonAltAzAt` returns altitude ≤ 0°, the moon contributes zero lux and the annotation does NOT render (no bracket fires). A separate one-line state appears: *"Moon below horizon — no moonlight at this time."*

   Crossover with /daylight: a link card *"Plan a pre-dawn walk → /daylight"*.

6. **Lunar standstill time-machine** — sibling to /sunpath's Stonehenge-through-time. The moon's declination swings between ±18.3° (minor standstill) and ±28.5° (major standstill) over the **18.6-year lunar nodal precession cycle** (NOT the Metonic cycle, which is the 19-year solar–lunar repeat — different phenomenon). Time slider: pick a year **within `[currentYear − 2000, currentYear + 2000]`** (Meeus low-precision lunar theory drifts ~1 standstill year per 1,000 years of extrapolation; ±2,000 keeps absolute error below one cycle). Show the nearest major/minor standstill at that epoch, with archaeoastronomy callouts (Callanish, Newgrange, Chimney Rock — when known sites' moon-alignments occurred). Reuses /sunpath's obliquity-aware Date-Through-Time pattern. A "accuracy envelope: ±1 year within ±2,000 yr; beyond range is illustrative only" footnote sits below the slider.

7. **Tide curve for ~12 baked coastal ports** — sine-wave plot of past 24h + next 24h tides at the nearest baked port. **v1 port list (post slice-0 audit):** Honolulu (canonical test-bed, replaces Yokohama), Cascais (replaces Lisbon), A Coruña, San Sebastián, Saint-Malo (replaces Brest), Boston, San Francisco, Auckland, Port Elizabeth (replaces Cape Town), Newlyn (replaces Dover), Karachi (replaces Mumbai), Sydney (Fort Denison). **Tide model:** harmonic synthesis from the 10 dominant constituents per port (M2 / S2 / N2 / K1 / O1 / P1 / Q1 / K2 / M4 / MS4 — amplitudes + phases hand-curated from public NOAA tide tables or UK Admiralty data). Annotation: *"Spring tides this week"* or *"Neap tides — sun and moon pull at right angles"* tied to current lunar phase. **King-tide warning:** flag when perigee + new/full moon align in the next 30 days.

8. **ICS calendar exports** — bake-time-generated static files:
   - `/moonpath/full-moons-<year>.ics` — **all** full moons that fall in the given Gregorian calendar year (12 or 13; 13 occurs about every 2.7 years when an extra synodic month fits in a calendar year — colloquially "blue moon" year). One VEVENT per moon. Standstills are NOT included (D15 — they're weeks-long viewer events, not point calendar entries). Two files baked: current year + next year.
   - `/moonpath/tides-<port>.ics` — per baked port, next 30 days of king tides (or D12 stub if no king tide in window). One file per port, hand-named after the port (e.g., `/moonpath/tides-yokohama.ics`).
   - Format mirrors `/sunpath/turnings-2026.ics` (VCALENDAR + VEVENTs + VALARM at -P1D).

## Non-goals

- **Lunar / solar eclipse prediction.** Eclipses have heavy edge-case math (umbra/penumbra geometry, Saros cycles). v2-or-later.
- **Lunar mansions, nakshatras, or moon astrology.** Off-brand. Could be its own entry if culturally framed (e.g., "Japanese lunar calendar" or "Vedic nakshatras") but not bundled here.
- **Full TPXO global tide model.** 12 hand-baked ports is the v1 target. Global coverage is a v3 question after the substrate proves out.
- **The word "supermoon" in any user-facing copy.** Apparent-size dial is in scope; the term is not.
- **Live tide predictions in the open ocean.** The 12-port list is coastal harbours only. Mid-ocean tides require different math.
- **Real-time tide gauge data.** Pure model output. No NOAA station API integration.
- **Moonpath shadow direction at the user's location.** Pencilled for v2 (deferred from the stretched-v1 list shared in the dream session).
- **Libration mini-dial.** Deferred. Adds ~30 LOC of orbital geometry; ship without.
- **Walkable shoreline window** (e.g., Mont Saint-Michel tidal road timings). Coastal-specific, deferred.
- **Bioluminescence / firefly windows.** Off-axis. Could be a future phenology entry.
- **/moonpath/<port>/ subpages.** Per-port pages were tried for /daylight and were removed post-launch as redundant. /moonpath is hub-only, URL-param routing.

## Decisions locked during spec authoring

- **D1 — Page name + URL.** `/moonpath/`, mirroring `/sunpath/`. NOT `/moonview/` despite the user's working name — pair-shape with sunpath is more valuable than asymmetric naming.
- **D2 — Tide model.** Harmonic synthesis from 10 constituents per port, hand-baked from public data sources. NOT a global model. NOT a live API.
- **D3 — Port list (provisional, gated on Q1 licensing).** Yokohama, Lisbon, A Coruña, San Sebastián, Brest, Boston, San Francisco, Auckland, Cape Town, Dover, Mumbai, Sydney. 12 ports, mix of pilgrimage-relevant and global-significance coasts. **Locked only after Q1 confirms each port's tide-constituent data is under an open-data license we can bake into the repo.** Any port that fails licensing review is swapped during slice-2 implementation, not now.
- **D4 — Geolocation discipline.** Same as /daylight D3 — explicit "use my location" button only. Never silent auto-geolocate.
- **D5 — Time zone resolution.** /moonpath is coord-driven, not stage-driven, so tz comes from the user's lat/lon (looked up via the same IANA approach as /daylight). Coord-without-resolvable-tz fallback: browser-local with `(local time)` label — see D18.
- **D6 — "Supermoon" word ban.** Tracked here so future contributors don't reintroduce it.
- **D7 — Earthshine threshold.** Lit fraction in `[0.03, 0.15]`. Below 0.03 the moon is too thin to see; above 0.15 earthshine is overpowered by the lit crescent.
- **D8 — Standstill time-slider range.** ±2,000 years from current year (revised from the dream-session ±5,000 after the doc-review surfaced Meeus low-precision drift). Standstills have ~18.6-year period so this gives ~215 standstills to scrub through — covers historical archaeoastronomy spans (Callanish ~1500 BCE, Newgrange ~3200 BCE both just inside range) without overrunning the lunar-theory accuracy envelope.

- **D9 — Tide section visibility threshold.** Hide the tide section entirely when the nearest baked port is **>200 km** from the user's coord. Inland coords + mid-ocean coords get a quiet "tides not applicable here" line, no chart. Resolves Q6 inline.

- **D10 — Spring/neap annotation thresholds.** Use **days from nearest syzygy** (new or full moon) to pick state:
   - 0–2 days: *"Spring tides this week (<new|full> moon)"*
   - 3–4 days: *"Tide range trending toward neap"*
   - 5–9 days: *"Neap tides — sun and moon pull at right angles"*
   - 10–11 days: *"Tide range trending toward spring"*
   - 12–14 days: *"Spring tides approaching"*
   Five states, deterministic, single rule.

- **D11 — ICS bake cadences (split by file type).**
   - **Full-moons ICS**: `full-moons-<year>.ics` for the current year + next year, rebaked annually (every January via CI cron or manual). Static across the year. Contains **all** full moons in the calendar year — 12 in most years, 13 in "blue moon" years (e.g., 2026 has 13 full moons). The bake script must not hardcode a `length === 12` assertion.
   - **Tide-port ICS**: `tides-<port>.ics`, rolling 30-day-from-bake-time windows, rebaked weekly via CI cron (separate work) so the data never goes more than 7 days stale.
   - **Standstill VEVENTs are NOT included in full-moons ICS** — see D15 below.

- **D12 — Empty-ICS fallback.** If a tide-port window contains zero king or extreme tides, the bake script writes a **single stub VEVENT** with SUMMARY *"No king tides this month at <port>"* and DTSTART = bake-date + 7 days (midnight UTC) + DTEND = +1 hour. Keeps the file RFC 5545 valid for strict parsers (Outlook, Apple Calendar) and gives subscribers a non-empty calendar.

- **D13 — Earthshine "lit fraction" semantics.** Use the Meeus illuminated-disk fraction `k = (1 − cos(2π · phase)) / 2`, where `phase` is the synodic phase 0..1 returned by the existing `moonPhaseAtUTC`. Sanity checks: new moon (phase=0) → k=0; first quarter (phase=0.25) → k=0.5; full moon (phase=0.5) → k=1; last quarter (phase=0.75) → k=0.5. The earthshine annotation fires when `0.03 ≤ k ≤ 0.15`.

- **D14 — Apparent-size rounding.** Always **one decimal place**, e.g., `"4.3% larger"`. Never `"4%"` or `"4.27%"`.

- **D15 — Standstill events are viewer-only, not calendar.** Lunar standstills are weeks-long extrema of an 18.6-year envelope, not point-in-time events. They render in the standstill time-machine widget (Feature 6) but are NOT included as VEVENTs in `full-moons-<year>.ics`. Future v2 may ship a separate `lunar-standstills.ics` if standstills-as-calendar-events surfaces real demand.

- **D16 — Apparent-size math is angular-diameter-based, not distance-based.** The dial computes `(meanDistance / todayDistance − 1) × 100` to give percentage offset in **apparent angular diameter** (which scales as 1/distance), not raw distance offset. Mean distance: 384,400 km. Example: at perigee 356,500 km → `(384400/356500 − 1) × 100 ≈ +7.8%` larger.

- **D17 — King-tide flag visibility.** The king-tide flag only renders when the tide section is visible (per D9, nearest baked port within 200 km). When tide section is hidden, king-tide flag is also hidden. No "global tide alerts for inland viewers."

- **D18 — Coord-without-IANA-tz fallback.** When the user supplies a coord whose IANA tz cannot be resolved at runtime (rare — only if the lat/lon-to-tz lookup library returns null), fall back to browser-local time with an explicit `(local time)` label, mirroring /daylight's D4 custom-route fallback. This is NOT a "custom-route" fallback (no such thing on /moonpath) but a "coord-without-tz" fallback.

## Open questions

**Blocking (must resolve before implementation):**

- **Q1 — Tide constants data source + licensing.** NOAA tide constituents for US ports are public domain. UK Admiralty / Japanese JMA / Australian BoM data licensing varies. Need to verify each of the 12 ports has constituent data we can legally bake into the repo. If 2-3 ports have restrictive licensing, swap them for alternates. *(Suggested resolution: build a short list of "verified open-data ports" first; the 12 in the spec are aspirational not committed.)*

- **Q2 — Moon altitude/azimuth precision target.** Meeus low-precision gives ±0.5° altitude. The dome diagram is rendered at ~300px wide; 0.5° is ~1px error. Acceptable? Or do we need Meeus high-precision (±0.05°, ~5× more code)? *(Suggested: low-precision is fine for visual rendering; the existing daylight v2 slice 1 already adopted ±15min moonrise tolerance with low-precision, consistent here.)*

**Non-blocking (resolve during implementation):**

- **Q3** — Palette for the brightness ring. /sunpath uses Stamen-grade muted; /daylight uses a slightly warmer terracotta. Moonpath could use a third axis (silvers / cools) or inherit from /sunpath. Decide during launch smoke.

- **Q4** — Should the page render in star mode (constellation) differently from light mode? The moon's visible against a starfield in star mode looks great visually; in light mode the dome diagram needs sky-blue treatment. Both should work; tune in launch smoke.

- **Q5** — Standstill callouts beyond the 3 named sites (Callanish, Newgrange, Chimney Rock). Should there be a fuller archaeoastronomy table (Stonehenge, Machu Picchu, Angkor Wat)? Probably yes but the 3 named are MVP. Add more during launch.

## Risks

- **Risk:** Tide harmonic synthesis from 10 constituents is good to ±0.3 m for most "interesting" tide moments but can drift on storm-affected days. *Mitigation:* document the model's design accuracy in a footnote on the tide section; never claim navigational accuracy.

- **Risk:** Hand-curating 12 ports' constituents is tedious and error-prone. *Mitigation:* unit-test each port against ≥5 NOAA-published reference heights; fail-loud at bake time if any port's predictions exceed ±0.5 m vs reference. Source URLs cited in the data file.

- **Risk:** Moon altitude math may have edge cases at extreme latitudes (the moon is circumpolar for parts of the lunar month at >75° lat). *Mitigation:* test at Tromsø (69.6°N) and Ushuaia (54.8°S); document that the page is informational, not navigational, beyond ±70° lat.

- **Risk:** Earthshine threshold `[0.03, 0.15]` is brand-voice prose-anchored, not astronomical. Some viewers might still see earthshine at 0.18 (in clear desert sky). *Mitigation:* the threshold is a public-facing approximation, not a research claim; documented in code comments.

- **Risk:** Standstill archaeoastronomy callouts may be culturally sensitive (especially Chimney Rock — Pueblo / Ancestral Puebloan sacred site). *Mitigation:* reference Wikipedia article links rather than making interpretive claims; note that lunar alignments are observed phenomena, not assertions about cultural intent.

- **Risk:** Adding a third almanac entry while only 2 are live (and sunpath has been live for a while; daylight just landed) front-loads work without confirming the slow-patience hypothesis is paying off. *Mitigation:* the moonpath substrate (moon math + tide math + ICS export tooling) is reusable for future entries (e.g., a tide-clock for Mont Saint-Michel or a phenology entry that uses moon math for nocturnal birds). Even if /moonpath itself doesn't draw traffic, the substrate compounds.

## Substrate notes (for future entries)

The /moonpath substrate adds two reusable building blocks:

- **Lunar ephemeris** (moonAltAzAt, moonDistanceAt, lunarStandstillNear) in `js/sunpath-math.js`. Future entries that need lunar context (nocturnal phenology, tide variants, archaeoastronomy widgets) reuse these.
- **Tide harmonic synthesis** in `js/tide-math.js` with a JSON port file. Adding a new port = appending one record to `assets/moonpath/tide-ports.json` and one fixture to the test. Future entries (Mont Saint-Michel walkable-window, Inland Sea pilgrim coves) reuse the synth.
- **ICS export bake-script pattern** (`scripts/bake-moonpath-ics`). Same shape as `scripts/bake-daylight-routes` — readable from any future entry that wants calendar exports (festival calendars, phenology calendars, etc.).

Three reusable layers, one shipped page. The slow-patience compound is what justifies entry #3 even before #2 (daylight) has been live a month.
