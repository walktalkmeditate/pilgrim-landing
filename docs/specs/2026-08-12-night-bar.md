## Problem

`/daylight` (renamed "The Light Budget" partway through this branch — D7) answered one question: does the light hold between sunrise and sunset? Real stages don't respect that window. The enrichment spec named pre-dawn starts as common on long stages back in May; a slow stage runs past sunset into blue hour. The bar had no way to draw any of that. `utcToBarX` mapped every instant onto `[sunrise, sunset]` and clamped — nothing before sunrise or after sunset could render at any width greater than zero. The three twilight bands the enrichment spec had already asked for existed in the code and drew nothing (see "What this cost, and why").

**Relationship to the darkness gate.** The darkness-data-audit gate (2026-08-11) names "the night bar" as the first of four downstream slices and states that three of the four consume a per-kilometre darkness value from `assets/darkness/*.json`. This branch is deliberately the fourth: nothing here reads that artifact. `grep -rn "darkness" js/ daylight/ css/` (excluding tests) turns up nothing from this branch. The audit measures how dark the sky actually is, glow by glow, along a route; this branch answers a narrower, purely astronomical question — when does true night begin and end here, and what is the moon doing inside it — using only the site's existing solar/lunar ephemeris math (`js/sunpath-math.js`, extended by the May enrichment; `js/moon-lux.js`, new here). The darkness ribbon remains a separate, future slice.

## Decisions

### D1 — The bar's domain widens to the full twilight sequence, with a margin

The bar's time axis widens from `[sunrise, sunset]` to `[earliest twilight − 60 min, latest twilight + 60 min]`, with a fallback chain — astronomical → nautical → civil → sunrise/sunset — for latitudes where deeper twilight never occurs.

`barDomainUTC(output)` (`js/daylight-math.js:204-217`) takes the earliest of `[astronomicalDawn, nauticalDawn, civilDawn, sunriseUTC]` and the latest of the dusk equivalents: "earliest/latest available," not "earliest/latest that exists everywhere." Above roughly 48° latitude in summer, astronomical (and sometimes nautical) twilight never occurs and those fields come back `null` from `SunPathMath`. The site's seven baked routes sit at 33–43°N, where the full chain always resolves, but `/daylight` also accepts arbitrary custom coordinates, so the fallback isn't decorative — see AC #14 (Finding 14) for two real coordinates that actually hit it.

Two alternatives were considered and rejected:

- **A full 24-hour axis.** Every day becomes directly comparable at a glance. But the walk — the bar's actual subject, and the reason the page exists — would shrink to roughly a quarter of the bar's width inside a full day, and the thing pilgrims open the page to read would suffer for a comparison nobody asked for.
- **Astronomical-only, with no margin.** The smallest possible change: extend the axis exactly to `[astronomicalDawn, astronomicalDusk]` and stop. But then true dark ends exactly at the bar's pixel edge — no dark is ever visible as a real span, only as a boundary, so the bar still can't answer the question this branch exists to answer: is there a night here, and how long is it.

The margin itself moved once during the branch: `fea6d0c` shipped it at 30 minutes; `4c02070` widened it to 60 (D5 explains why).

### D2 — The margin is conditional per side

The margin is added only where astronomical twilight itself exists for that side (`js/daylight-math.js:210-211`: `astronomicalDawn ? BAR_DOMAIN_MARGIN_MS : 0`, and the dusk equivalent). The alternative — letting the domain edge, and therefore the true-dark segment, fall back to whichever rung the domain itself used (nautical or civil) — was rejected because labelling nautical or civil space "true dark" overstates the darkness: nautical twilight is still meaningfully lit, just past a −12° gate rather than a −18° one, and calling it "true dark" would be a false claim, not a subtler one.

`ce22b20`'s finding: at Stockholm and Paris on the 2026 summer solstice, and Reykjavik the day before, astronomical twilight never occurs, and an *unconditional* margin used to widen the domain past whichever rung it fell back to — rendering nothing in that sliver, since neither the true-dark segment nor the adaptation mark draws without `astronomicalDawn`/`Dusk`. After the fix, `dl-bar-civil` — the outermost band that actually exists at Stockholm — spans the full bar, `BAR_X1` to `BAR_X2` exactly. The edge means something again instead of padding into dead space.

### D3 — Twilight band strokes use `butt` caps, not `round`

At `stroke-width: 10`, a round cap adds a radius's worth of overhang — about 5px — past each declared endpoint. While every band shared its endpoints with its neighbours (the pre-fix, geometrically-degenerate state described below), the overhang was invisible: five overlapping 5px overhangs at the same two points look like nothing. Once the bands nested at distinct widths (`56bb389`), the same overhang bled real bands into each other — true dark painted roughly 26.5px against a 16.5px nominal width, about 10px of that visibly bleeding into the astronomical band beside it, blurring exactly the boundaries this change exists to reveal. `css/daylight.css`'s five nested band classes (`.dl-bar-daylight`, `.dl-bar-civil`, `.dl-bar-nautical`, `.dl-bar-astronomical`, `.dl-bar-truedark`) now all use `stroke-linecap: butt`. `.dl-bar-walk` (the walk-window segment) keeps its round cap on purpose — a deliberately rounded end on one independent segment, not a boundary between two things that need to read as distinct.

### D4 — The moon-lantern band is gated on solar altitude

The moon-lantern band (`computeMoonBandRuns`, `js/daylight.js`, commented "(D4)" at line 503) skips samples between `civilDawn` and `civilDusk` — falling back to `sunriseUTC`/`sunsetUTC` when civil twilight doesn't exist, the same fallback `barDomainUTC` already uses.

Before this gate, the band checked only lunar altitude, so it painted "usable light along an open trail" (or brighter) whenever the moon happened to be up — including in broad daylight. Replicated across all 365 days of 2026 at Burgos: 423 segments wider than 20px were more than half inside daylight. The reproduction case, 2026-09-19, painted a "mid" bracket run beginning at 14:52 UTC and running into the afternoon, well before that day's 18:19:43Z sunset — moonlight offered as the light a walker is using, at an hour nobody needs it. `js/daylight-render.test.js`'s Finding-9 section now sweeps the same 365 days and asserts zero overlapping segments (was 423). `js/moon-lux.js` itself was never wrong — its lux math was correct throughout; the caller's domain was wrong.

### D5 — Dark adaptation is marked at 20 minutes after true dark begins

`DARK_ADAPT_MIN = 20` (`js/daylight.js:27`, commented "(D5)") — the rough figure for rod-cell dark adaptation, a walker's number no astronomy tool shows because a camera never needs it. The mark sits at `astronomicalDusk + DARK_ADAPT_MIN`; the domain itself ends at `astronomicalDusk + BAR_DOMAIN_MARGIN_MS` (D1). Both anchor to the same instant, `astronomicalDusk`, so the mark lands a fixed `BAR_DOMAIN_MARGIN_MS − DARK_ADAPT_MIN` short of the right edge at every latitude and season, by construction (`js/daylight.js:867-876` says so in a comment now, so it doesn't get "fixed" again).

At the original 30-minute margin the gap was 10 minutes — the mark measured at x = 570.0–570.5px on a bar ending at 576, visually crushed against the frame. Widening the margin to 60 (D1) is what gives the 20-minute gap room to read: the render harness now checks the mark clears the edge by at least 20px at five latitude/hemisphere combinations (Burgos, the equator, Santiago, Tokyo, Ushuaia).

### D6 — Moon-lux math moves to its own module

`moonLuxAt`, `luxBracketFor`, and `kFromPhase` were extracted from `js/moonpath.js` (~68 KB) into `js/moon-lux.js` (3.4 KB), so `/daylight` loads exactly the two functions and one formula it needs, not the tide curve, the lunar-standstill time machine, or the eclipse pointer that come with the rest of `moonpath.js`. `js/moonpath.js` now aliases `luxBracketFor`/`moonLuxAt`/`kFromPhase` from the new module (`js/moonpath.js:250-262`) instead of keeping its own copies; every existing call site, including the exported `api.luxBracketFor`, is unchanged. `js/moonpath.test.js` passing unmodified at 362/362 is the proof the extraction preserved behaviour; `js/moon-lux.test.js` (20/20) proves the module directly, including the zero cases — moon below the horizon, new moon — the moon-lantern band (D4) leans on.

This supersedes an explicit note that used to sit in `js/moonpath.js`: the illuminated-disk-fraction formula was inlined identically in both `js/daylight.js` and `js/moonpath.js`, the latter carrying a comment declining to promote it to a shared helper because it was "not a slice-1 helper." That reasoning predated `js/moon-lux.js` existing at all. Now that a shared moon-maths module exists, a one-line formula duplicated in two files is exactly what belongs in it — the two call sites can no longer quietly drift apart.

### D7 — The page is renamed "The Light Budget"; the URL is unchanged

"Daylight Walk Budget" named the mechanism (a budget, calculated from daylight); "The Light Budget" names the thing a walker actually spends. The rename touches `<title>`, `og:title`, `twitter:title`, the JSON-LD `WebPage` name and `BreadcrumbList` entry, the `<h1>`, the footer's "X is part of Pilgrim" line, and the two other pages that name this one in running prose (`/moonpath`'s pre-dawn crosslink, `/sunpath`'s "if you're walking a pilgrimage stage" crosslink). Left alone, on purpose: the URL (`/daylight`), the almanac footer nav link text ("Daylight," `daylight/index.html:239`), and `llms.txt`'s lowercase functional description — none of those were the page's proper name in the first place, so none needed to change.

The ICS `PRODID` (`js/daylight-math.js`: `-//Pilgrim//Daylight Walk Budget//EN` → `-//Pilgrim//The Light Budget//EN`) was updated too — correcting a belief that briefly went into a commit message as fact: that changing it would orphan live calendar subscriptions. It doesn't. A subscribed calendar event's identity lives in the feed URL and each `VEVENT`'s `UID` (`buildICS`'s `uid = 'daylight-' + fmtUTCInstant(startUTC) + '@pilgrimapp.org'`) — `PRODID` is a generator identifier, informational only, never surfaced to a reader. `js/daylight-math.test.js:201` pins the new string.

### D8 — The new facts are surfaced as text, not only geometry

Two problems made this necessary rather than optional. First, the bar's `role="img"` flattens its SVG subtree for assistive technology, so even the in-bar "eyes adjust" label was never exposed to a screen reader. Second, the accessible name itself (`aria-label`, mirrored from a `<title>` element `renderSVG` builds, `js/daylight.js:664-680`) used to be finalized from sunrise/sunset alone, before the moon-band sampling ever ran — it structurally could not describe true dark, the moon band, or the adaptation mark.

`titleText` now grows one clause per new element, each gated on exactly the field that decides whether `renderSVG` draws the corresponding thing: true dark's range only when both `astronomicalDawn`/`Dusk` exist (falling back to "begins at" / "until" when only one does), "partly moonlit" only when `computeMoonBandRuns` actually produced a visible run, the adaptation time only when `astronomicalDusk` exists. The sentence never claims more, or less, than what's on the bar.

Outside the SVG, `recompute()` pushes a plain-language annotation ("True dark holds from … to …; your eyes will have adjusted by …", `js/daylight.js:196-229`) gated on the same fields, and carries a `moonBrightnessAtAdapt` field forward — `MoonLux.luxBracketFor`'s own `.prose` string, not a rewritten copy of it. The moon legend (the DOM's existing "here's what the sky is doing" channel) reuses that same prose, following `/moonpath`'s own lux-ring precedent of pairing a bracket label with prose rather than a raw lux figure: this page speaks in walking terms, not photometric ones.

## What this cost, and why

**The May bug.** The enrichment spec (`docs/specs/2026-05-13-daylight-enrichment.md`, AC #2) specified three twilight bands in May. They shipped geometrically broken: `utcToBarX` clamped every instant to `[sunrise, sunset]`, so astronomical dawn (before sunrise) and astronomical dusk (after sunset) both clamped to the same two x-coordinates as sunrise and sunset themselves — all three bands rendered at exactly the daylight band's width, identical to each other and to the track underneath them. The May 13 launch doc (`docs/specs/2026-05-13-daylight-enrichment-launch.md`) recorded this as a success: *"Twilight bands: so faint they read as atmosphere, not features. The existing daylight band still defines the bar's silhouette."* A thirty-minute fresh-eyes visual pass cannot distinguish "correctly subtle" from "geometrically degenerate" — both look like faint bands sitting quietly behind the daylight band. The bug then sat for three months, from mid-May until this branch touched the same code in August.

**The same class of error, in reverse, on this branch.** `fea6d0c` widened the domain and moved eight `utcToBarX` call sites onto it, and the change was described, accurately as far as it went, as "the twilight gradient finally nests." The band x-coordinates were correct. But three more draw sites — the daylight band's own fill, the sunrise/sunset ticks and labels (including the duplicated reverse-mode branch), and the reverse-mode buffer band — were still built from the raw `BAR_X1`/`BAR_X2` pixel-edge literals, which used to equal sunrise/sunset exactly back when the domain *was* `[sunrise, sunset]`, and silently didn't anymore. The daylight band painted full-width at 0.22 alpha, last in the draw order, directly over the now-correctly-nested twilight bands underneath it — civil twilight and high noon composited to the identical `rgb(219,213,203)`. The nesting was real in the DOM and invisible on screen, painted over by something that looked, by every geometric check performed at the time, like it had nothing to do with the bug.

Nine reviewers (`/ce-code-review`, run `20260812-103004-6d7b0175`, 16 findings at or above the confidence gate) plus a render-test harness (`js/daylight-render.test.js`, written the same session, red on 12 of its first 24 assertions) caught it. Not a visual pass.

**The lesson, stated plainly: SVG geometry needs coordinate assertions, not eyeballing.** A band's x-coordinates being correct and a band being *visible* are two different claims, and only the second one is what a reader sees. This is why `js/daylight-render.test.js` exists — it now carries 65 assertions, all green, and checks not just where things are drawn but what draws on top of them.

## Outstanding

**No browser render has been performed.** Every verification cited above — for this spec and for the branch as a whole — is numeric and test-based: a fake-DOM harness recording SVG attributes, WCAG contrast arithmetic against parsed CSS, and pure-function assertions. Given the May precedent above, that is a deliberate limitation to name, not an oversight to gloss over. A real browser render, in all three themes, is the next and last step before this branch is truly done — tracked as the final slice in the plan.

## Carried forward

The widened contrast sweep (AC #13) checked only the bar's own `.dl-bar-label*` classes, because that's what this branch touches. Along the way it surfaced six pre-existing AA failures elsewhere on the page, all sharing the same shape — `color: var(--stone)` at a per-element opacity that composites well under 4.5:1 against `--parchment`.
